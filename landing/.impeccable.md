## Design Context

### Users
Three audiences, in priority order:
1. **Hedera hackathon judges** — DevRel engineers who reviewed the prior-round winners and already know this builder's metered-payments rail. They read on a laptop, during the day, evaluating many submissions fast. They want to see the service, the payment flow, and proof — not infra marketing.
2. **Developers building agents** — people running Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw or Hermes who need their agent to rent a computer mid-task without an account or API key. They read docs-first and distrust hype.
3. **Self-hosting agent operators** — the OpenClaw/Grok Bot crowd running agents 24/7 on a VPS; account-averse, cost-sensitive, want to *watch* the agent work.

Job to be done: understand in under a minute that an agent can rent a browser, a Linux machine or a full desktop by the second, pay from its own wallet, and that every second and every file it produced is verifiable.

### Brand Personality
**Plain. Measured. Provable.**
Plainspoken in the Fly.io register — short sentences, concrete nouns, no adjectives doing the work. "Measured" in both senses: metered to the second, and unhurried in tone. "Provable" because the whole thesis is that nobody has to take our word for the bill or the output.
Emotional goal: the calm confidence of a utility bill you can audit, not the excitement of a launch. The one line everything traces back to: *a rented computer is the one product where the meter and the payment are the same event.*

### Aesthetic Direction
**Structure from Runloop, showcases from Solari, meaning from Kleeto.**
- Light page ground (derived from context: judges and devs reading during the day, evaluating — light reads as legible and confident). Not pure white; tinted toward the brand hue.
- **Dark showcase cards** sit on the light ground — this is Runloop's own dark-card-on-light pattern, and it is where Solari's animations live: the aurora sweep, the 3D carousel with in-card loops, the RUNNING→PAUSED→RESUMING terminal, the Bézier cursor, the amber benchmark bars.
- **Accent: amber** (`#f5b301` family). It is what makes the aurora, the bar charts and the carousel underline read; it reads as gold, which suits a product whose subject is money; and it replaces Runloop's forest green everywhere so light ground and dark cards share one accent.
- Runloop's structural rhythm throughout: small uppercase pill eyebrow above every section, a single **boxed highlight word** in headings, generous radii (8/16/24/40), tight small body type, one full-bleed dark CTA band.
- References: runloop.ai (rhythm), getsolari.com (showcases). Anti-references: infrastructure b-roll (pool diagrams, telemetry dashboards), glassmorphism, neon-on-black, the "big number + gradient" hero metric template, any crypto-native visual language (coins, chains, purple-blue gradients).
- Fonts: **not** Inter, IBM Plex, Space Grotesk, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta or any other training-data default. Brand words for selection: plain / measured / provable → think meter face, utility bill, receipt printer. Tabular figures are mandatory: the product is numbers.

### Design Principles
1. **The meter is the product.** Cost appears in human units ("ten minutes on a desktop: 2.5 cents"), never as `$0.0000045/GiB/s`. A meter ticks somewhere on the page at all times.
2. **Proof beats claims.** Every performance number is measured and says so; every artifact is hashed; the receipt is a first-class section, not a footnote.
3. **Show the machine, never the infrastructure.** Real screenshots of real desktops and browsers, animations of what an agent actually does. No pool diagrams, no cloud icons.
4. **Plain English in the hero, protocol words in the docs.** No "on-chain", "gas", "facilitator", "permissionless" above the fold. "No account, no API key, no card on file" is the phrasing.
5. **Motion conveys state.** Running, paused, settled, revealed. Loops pause off-screen. Nothing animates for decoration.
